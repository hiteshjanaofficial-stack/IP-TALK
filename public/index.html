a small relay for a simple idea

Three strangers, three networks, one room.

One person is on office wifi. Another is on mobile data across the country. A third is behind a home router that's never let anything in. None of that matters here — they type the same room number and room IP, pick a name, and they're in the same conversation.

How a room comes together

  1. 1

    Agree on a room number and room IP

    Whoever starts the conversation picks a room number and a room IP and shares both with everyone who should join — over text, email, wherever.

  2. 2

    Everyone enters the same two values, with any username

    No accounts, no invite links to click through. Each person just needs a name for themselves — it only has to be unique inside that room.

  3. 3

    The room opens, wherever people are

    Office wifi, home broadband, mobile data — the room doesn't care. Messages arrive for everyone in it, live.

A quick honest note: a browser can't dial an arbitrary IP address directly — no website can, that door is closed for everyone's protection. So the "room IP" you enter works as a second shared key, alongside the room number, that this server uses to place everyone in the correct room. It's the same idea video-call apps use behind the scenes, just written in plain terms here.
